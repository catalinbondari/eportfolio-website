What the research did to the expected returns

Expected returns start at CAPM — beta, net of charges and the wrapper’s tax — which can see risk, correlation and cost and no edge in any company. The accounts, the insider filings and the valuation gap are blended in as Black-Litterman views: each sized by the published effect, halved because premia shrink after publication, and shrunk further towards this book’s own scored record as it accumulates. Low confidence on purpose.

What a target actually requires

What compounds fastest

Money compounds, so what gets maximised is expected log return: growth ≈ return − volatility²∕2. That second term is why a lower-returning mix can leave you richer, and why past a point more risk makes you poorer.

Hedges that hold up when it matters

Ranked by what each does to compounding at its own best size — not by how much volatility it removes, because a hedge that costs more return than the variance it saves makes you poorer slowly. The last two columns are the honest test: correlation on ordinary days against correlation on your worst 10%.

How to get there

The trades that move today's book toward the default mix — your Buy / Hold / Sell calls on the companies, funds for the rest — cut to what one month can actually do given your cash and free sells. Trend does not change the destination — it only decides which trades go first.

Risk surfaces

Two shapes a flat chart cannot show. The blend surface sweeps every mix of two funds so you can see the whole trade-off at once instead of reading it off eighteen table rows. The correlation surface plots each holding's correlation to the rest of the book through time — because diversification is not a number, it is a number that moves, and it moves most at exactly the moment you were relying on it. Drag to rotate, scroll to zoom.

Mortgage deposit

Money with a deadline is a different kind of money. Its job is to be intact and available on the date, not to compound — so it sits outside the investable book, out of every weight and risk figure, and nothing here will ever suggest investing it.

Pension

Kept apart from the tradable books on purpose. The money is not accessible, most scheme funds have no market ticker, and folding the pot into your weights would inflate every cap and budget that depends on the tradable total. It still counts towards what you have.

Leverage, and where it stops working

The obvious shortcut, and the reason it is not one. Borrowing multiplies the return in proportion and the drag by the square, so growth turns over at a point this book's own numbers decide.

Options — the only convex instrument on this page

Priced with Black–Scholes at each line's own realised volatility, then re-read under its CAPM drift rather than the risk-neutral one the pricing model assumes. The gap between those two is the whole argument.

The run you would have to win

No single option, at any strike anyone will sell you, covers the multiple the target needs. So it has to be a sequence — and the probability of a sequence is the product.
